“Engineering” isn’t one career. It’s two dozen.

The book maps the five engineering families and the 24 careers inside them. Every career gets its own full-color pages, laid out the same way so a student can compare them.

For every career

  • What the job really is, in plain English
  • How the pay compares and how fast the field is changing
  • Where you’ll work: office, field, lab or factory
  • The skills and personality traits it rewards
  • An hour-by-hour “Day in the Life”

The questions students don’t know to ask

  • What makes a good engineer (temperament, not just math grades)
  • How to size up a college engineering program
  • What a PE license means for your career
  • How internships and mentors really work

Written by someone who has done the job

Jim Martin, PhD, PE, is a licensed professional engineer and president of Design Engineering, Inc., a civil engineering firm in Metairie, Louisiana, that works on bridges, drainage, roadways, levees and pump stations.

He started as a drafter and rodman, has taught engineering at the University of New Orleans for twenty years, and is a Scoutmaster. He chose engineering in high school without ever asking what engineers do every day, and wrote the book he wishes someone had handed him.
